## Project Description

In this project, we compare three open-source decoder-only LLMs—GPT-2, GPT-Neo 1.3B, and GPT-2-Medium—on creative text generation.  
Our goal is to evaluate how well these models handle narrative prompts, general reasoning, and dialogue-style questions.

**Novelty:**  
Unlike typical model evaluations that focus on benchmarks, our project evaluates *creative* behavior, coherence, hallucination, and conversational stability using a small, controlled prompt set of 15 items.

**Motivation:**  
Decoder-only transformers often behave very differently in free-form text generation tasks. Understanding these differences helps explain model strengths, hallucination tendencies, and limitations in narrative reasoning.

We also review related work in creativity evaluation for LLMs and discuss challenges such as hallucination, repetition, and lack of grounding.


## Methods

### **Models Evaluated**
- GPT-2 (117M)
- GPT-2-Medium (345M)
- GPT-Neo 1.3B

All models were run locally via HuggingFace `pipeline` using CPU-only inference due to hardware limits.

### **Prompt Set (15 items)**
We used a fixed set of 15 prompts covering:
- Creative story openings  
- Dialogue-style Q&A  
- Factual knowledge  
- Instruction following  

### **Generation Settings**
- `max_length = 200`
- `num_return_sequences = 1`
- `set_seed(42)` for reproducibility

### **Evaluation Criteria**
We evaluated each output for:
- Fluency  
- Coherence  
- Creativity  
- Hallucination  
- Repetition / degeneracy  
- Dialogue correctness  
- Factuality (for factual prompts)


## Results

### **1. Creativity & Coherence**
- GPT-Neo produced the most coherent long-form narratives.
- GPT-2 showed frequent repetition and unrelated digressions.
- GPT-2-Medium improved over GPT-2 but still lacked consistency.

### **2. Factuality**
- All three models hallucinated factual answers.
- GPT-2 often produced completely unrelated answers.

### **3. Dialogue Stability**
- GPT-2 frequently treated itself as multiple speakers.
- GPT-Neo produced more stable assistant-style responses.

### **Overall Trends**
| Model | Creativity | Coherence | Factuality | Dialogue Stability | Repetition |
|------|-----------|-----------|------------|---------------------|------------|
| GPT-2 | Low | Low | Poor | Poor | High |
| GPT-2-Medium | Medium | Medium | Poor | Medium | Medium |
| GPT-Neo 1.3B | High | High | Moderate | High | Low |


## Error Analysis

We selected three representative failure cases.

### **Error 1 — Hallucinated Dialogue (GPT-2)**  
Prompt:  
`"User: I’ve been feeling overwhelmed with college lately. Assistant:"`

GPT-2 invented random campus-related dialogue, repeated phrases, and produced contradictory statements.

**Issue:** No dialogue grounding; model cannot maintain consistent persona.

---

### **Error 2 — Repetition Loop (GPT-2)**  
Prompt:  
`"User: Imagine you are a time traveler from 2200. Introduce yourself."`

GPT-2 repeatedly outputs:  
“I don’t understand that.”  
for 10–12 lines.

**Issue:** Degenerative repetition, a common problem in small decoder models.

---

### **Error 3 — Factual Hallucination (GPT-2)**  
Prompt:  
`"Who wrote Pride and Prejudice?"`

GPT-2 responded with unrelated dialogue about Boston and never answered "Jane Austen."

**Issue:** Failure to retrieve basic factual knowledge.

---

These examples highlight limitations of small, ungrounded models in both knowledge tasks and dialogue consistency.


## Limitations & Future Improvements

### **Limitations**
- CPU-only inference slowed experimentation.
- Large models (LLaMA-2-7B, NeoX-20B) couldn’t be tested locally due to hardware & gated access.
- No quantitative metric (e.g., creativity score, BLEU, ROUGE).

### **Future Work**
- Run evaluations on a GPU-based environment (Colab Pro / high-RAM machines).
- Include larger models like LLaMA-2-Chat and Mistral-7B for comparison.
- Add human evaluation surveys for creativity scoring.
- Use automatic metrics (perplexity, MAUVE score).
- Add fine-tuning on narrative datasets to test improvement.
