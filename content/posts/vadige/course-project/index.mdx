# Project Proposal

This project compares three open-source decoder-only language models — **GPT-2**, **GPT-2-Medium**, and **GPT-Neo 1.3B** — on creative text generation tasks.  
Our goal is to evaluate how well these models handle **narrative prompts, dialogue-style interactions, and simple factual questions**, while identifying patterns of hallucination, repetition, and coherence issues.

The task aligns with Statistical NLP by focusing on **autoregressive model behavior**, generative stability, and qualitative differences across architectures.

---

# Project Description

We evaluate three decoder-only transformer LLMs across a fixed set of **15 creative prompts**, including:

- Story openings  
- Dialogue continuations  
- Instruction-following prompts  
- Factual Q&A  

### Novelty  
Unlike benchmark-driven evaluations, this project emphasizes **creative behavior**, narrative flow, hallucination patterns, and conversational stability — areas where small and mid-sized LLMs differ significantly.

### Motivation  
Decoder-only LMs often behave unpredictably in free-form text generation.  
Understanding their weaknesses (e.g., repetition, missing grounding, invented dialogue) helps characterize model limitations and guide future improvements.

# Methodology

### Dataset / Prompt Design
We created a **15-prompt evaluation set** covering:
- Creative writing  
- Dialogue scenarios  
- Factual queries  
- Instruction following  

### Models Evaluated
- **GPT-2 (117M)**  
- **GPT-2-Medium (345M)**  
- **GPT-Neo 1.3B**  

All models were run locally using HuggingFace `pipeline()` on CPU-only inference.

### Generation Settings
- `max_length = 200`  
- `num_return_sequences = 1`  
- `set_seed(42)` for reproducibility  

### Evaluation Criteria
Each model output was analyzed for:
- Fluency  
- Coherence  
- Creativity  
- Factual accuracy  
- Hallucination  
- Dialogue stability  
- Repetition / degeneracy  

---

# Results

We compared model behavior across all prompts.

## **1. Creativity & Coherence**
- **GPT-Neo 1.3B**: Most coherent narratives; better long-range consistency.  
- **GPT-2-Medium**: Moderate coherence; better than GPT-2 but still unstable.  
- **GPT-2**: Struggled with narrative flow; frequent off-topic digressions.

## **2. Factuality**
- All models hallucinated frequently.
- **GPT-2** produced the most incorrect or unrelated answers.

## **3. Dialogue Stability**
- **GPT-Neo** maintained conversational structure best.  
- **GPT-2** confused speaker roles and repeated lines.

## Overall Performance Table

| Model         | Creativity | Coherence | Factuality | Dialogue Stability | Repetition |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|------------|--------------------|------------|
| GPT-2         | Low       | Low       | Poor       | Poor               | High       |
| GPT-2-Medium  | Medium    | Medium    | Poor       | Medium             | Medium     |
| GPT-Neo 1.3B  | High      | High      | Moderate   | High               | Low        |

# Error Analysis

We selected 3 representative failure cases.

### **Error 1 — Hallucinated Dialogue (GPT-2)**
**Prompt:**  
*“User: I’ve been feeling overwhelmed with college lately. Assistant:”*

GPT-2 invented multiple speakers, repeated phrases, and produced contradictory statements.

**Issue:** No grounding; unstable conversational modeling.

---

### **Error 2 — Repetition Loop (GPT-2)**
**Prompt:**  
*“User: Imagine you are a time traveler from 2200. Introduce yourself.”*

GPT-2 repeated the sentence **“I don’t understand that.”** more than 10 times.

**Issue:** Classic degenerative repetition in small models.

---

### **Error 3 — Factual Hallucination (GPT-2)**
**Prompt:**  
*“Who wrote Pride and Prejudice?”*

Instead of answering *Jane Austen*, GPT-2 generated unrelated dialogue about Boston.

**Issue:** Missing factual recall and over-generation.

These errors highlight the limitations of smaller decoder-only LLMs in narrative reasoning, factuality, and role stability.
