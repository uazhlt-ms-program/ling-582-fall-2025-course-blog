## Description

<p>This paper studies how the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP) has changed over many years. The authors collected around 80,000 research papers from the ACL Anthology, which is a large online library for NLP research. They used computer programs to read these papers and automatically group them into different categories, such as “language resources,” “text generation,” “applications,” and “linguistic analysis.”</p>
<p>After grouping the papers, they looked at how often each topic appeared across different years. They found that some topics became very popular over time, especially deep learning, transformer models, and text generation. Other areas, like old rule-based methods, became less common. They also saw that most research focuses on English, while many low-resource languages are rarely studied. The paper explains that important areas like fairness, ethics, and multimodal research still need more attention.</p>

#### What is the approach or innovation?

The authors collect a large set of ACL papers and design a taxonomy of fields of study, such as language resources, applications, generation, and analysis. They then use weakly supervised machine learning to assign each paper to one or more of these fields. Finally, they analyze how the number of papers in each field changes over time. The innovation is combining a custom taxonomy with large-scale automatic classification to study the entire field of NLP.

#### What are the results and how do they compare with competing approaches?

The results show that topics related to deep learning, transformers, and pre-training have grown very quickly, while rule-based and symbolic methods have decreased. The paper also shows that most NLP research focuses on English and that low-resource languages, fairness, and multimodal research are still under-represented. This is not a model-comparison paper, so there are no “baseline models,” but compared to earlier small surveys, this study uses much more data and gives a broader and more detailed view of the field.

#### What problems remain and what are the next steps?

Several problems remain. The study only uses ACL Anthology papers, so it may miss work from other conferences and domains. The taxonomy might need to be updated as new topics appear. The classification is weakly supervised, so some papers could be mis-labeled. Next steps include adding more data sources, refining the taxonomy, improving classification quality, and possibly building interactive tools to explore the landscape.
