## Description

This paper compares performance of LLMs to human annotators in analysing social-media content along different dimensions like sentiment, political leaning, emotional intensity and sarcasm. The authors collected online posts, had both humans and various LLMs label them and measure the level of agreement. The level of agreement between them is measured using statistical methods like the inter rater reliability calculated using the Krippendorff's alpha value and correlation values. The authors try to assess the potential of LLMs evaluation over human analysis across these dimensions. This is helpful to evaluate if LLMs are a viable substitute or supplements to humans in content analysis tasks.

#### What are the results and how do they compare with competing approaches?

##### Prompt

```markdown
What are the results and how do they compare with competing approaches?
```

The results show that Large Language Models (LLMs) can achieve levels of consistency comparable to or exceeding those of human annotators in certain dimensions.

Inter-rater reliability:

- Human annotators showed high reliability in sentiment analysis (alpha = 0.95) and moderate reliability in political leaning (alpha = 0.55), emotional intensity (alpha = 0.65), and sarcasm detection (alpha = 0.25).
- LLMs demonstrated high reliability in sentiment analysis (alpha = 0.95), political leaning (alpha = 0.80), and emotional intensity (alpha = 0.85), but low reliability in sarcasm detection (alpha = 0.25).

Temporal consistency:

- Most LLMs exhibited excellent temporal consistency across most dimensions, with Intra-Class Correlation Coefficients (ICCs) above 0.990 for sentiment analysis.
- ICCs ranged from 0.965 to 0.997 for political leaning, 0.966 to 0.996 for emotional intensity, and moderate consistency for sarcasm detection.

Comparison to human annotators:

- LLMs performed on par with humans in evaluating sentiment, with no significant differences in mean ratings (t-test, p = 0.277).
- LLMs showed comparable performance to humans in assessing political leaning, with no significant differences (t-test, p = 0.178).
- Humans rated emotional intensity higher than LLMs, with significant differences (t-test, p < 0.001).
- Both humans and LLMs struggled with sarcasm detection, providing statistically similar but variable ratings.

Comparison to competing approaches:

- The study's results are comparable to or better than those reported in previous studies on sentiment analysis, sarcasm detection, and emotional intensity assessment [2, 3, 4].
- The study's comprehensive evaluation of multiple LLMs and dimensions provides a more nuanced understanding of their strengths and limitations compared to human annotators.

Overall, the results suggest that LLMs can be effective in certain natural language processing tasks, but may not yet match human performance in more complex tasks like sarcasm detection and emotional intensity assessment.
