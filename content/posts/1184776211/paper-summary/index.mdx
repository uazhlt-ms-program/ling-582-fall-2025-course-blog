## Description

_In your own words, what is this paper about?_

The authors propose a method of identifying ambiguous words through
an unsupervised machine learning process. Intuitively, their goal is to capture
the intuition that any word's meaning can be represented by its context of
usage. This is, that the distinct meanings of ambiguous words can be captured
by classifying those contexts into groups of similarity.

Given a target word, their method uses the context of all occurrences of that
word. For each context, the trained CBOW embeddings of all context words within
*k*=5 are combined (by averaging) to calculate a vector representation of the
context. Those contexts are then grouped via DBSCAN (Density-Based Spatial
Clustering for Applications with Noise), thereby grouping distinct meanings of
the target word, while excluding non-informative contexts as noise.

Because DBSCAN relies on provided parameters—a nearness threshold for each
datapoint (epsilon) and a minumum number of near datapoints within that
threshold—which must be determined, Thurnbauer et al. ran the model through an
extensive combination of parameter settings and evalutated the results based
on `sci-kitlearn`'s `silhoutte_score()` function.

The method proved effective at distinguishing an ambiguous word's meanings,
demonstrated comparing the grouped context vectors to other words, yielding
similar and related other words.

## Motivation

_Why did you select this paper?_

The topic of unsupervised word-disambiguation appeals to me for the reason that
it pertains to a project that I've been tinkering with:
exploring ways to create an automated solver for the New York Times Games daily
Connections puzzle.
Not only is the Connections puzzle based in grouping of similar words, but
a challenge for human players is that many of the words included in any given
puzzle are ambiguous.

The work presented in the paper, i.e. identifying a word's multiple meanings
and subsequently grouping those distinct meanings with other related words,
is directly applicable to the Connections challenge.

#### What is the motivation for the work?


The motivation is to develop an **unsupervised, lightweight method** to resolve word ambiguity in natural language processing (NLP), leveraging **word embeddings** and **DBSCAN clustering**. Traditional approaches (e.g., Transformers) are too resource-intensive for real-time or sensitive environments, while supervised WSD methods require labeled data. The work aims to:  
1. Automatically identify ambiguous words and their meanings via **context vector clustering**.  
2. Provide a **parameter-optimized clustering strategy** to balance cluster quality and specificity.  
3. Enable **interpretable disambiguation** for practical applications like query resolution in search engines or chatbots.  
4. Address the challenge of most words being ambiguous, emphasizing the need for efficient, unsupervised solutions without labeled data.  

By focusing on **context-based clustering**, the authors aim to capture semantic coherence and practical relevance, offering a scalable, cost-effective alternative to complex models.
