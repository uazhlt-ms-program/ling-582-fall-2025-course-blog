## Description

Using images to achieve a multimodal approach, the authors of this paper tackle the challenge of capturing idiomatic representation in both English and Brazilian Portuguese. They did this in a 3-step approach: A) idiom detection, B) literal meaning generation of the idiomatic nominal compound (NC), and C) aligning images that best correspond to the intended interpretation of the NC. For Portuguese, they took a slightly different approach and tested two methods. In the first, once the idiom detection stage was completed, they translated the NC to English. In the second, the NC was not translated and the authors used multilingual multimodal image alignment models that could process multiple languages. The best results were achieved using model ensembling for both languages and Portuguese performed better without translation to English.

#### What problems remain and what are the next steps?

"Despite the progress shown in papers like the one we've discussed, significant challenges remain in idiom disambiguation and broader idiom interpretation. Here's a breakdown of the remaining problems and potential next steps for improvement:

**Remaining Problems:**

1.  **Contextual Sensitivity:** Even with powerful LLMs, accurately capturing the nuanced context required for disambiguation remains difficult. Subtle shifts in surrounding words can drastically change the idiom’s intended meaning.

2.  **Novel Idioms and Regional Variations:** NLP systems struggle with idioms that are not frequently encountered in training data, or with regional variations of idioms. The long tail of idioms presents a constant challenge.

3.  **Figurative Language Beyond Idioms:** Idioms are just one form of figurative language. Metaphors, similes, irony, and sarcasm all add layers of complexity to understanding natural language. A comprehensive solution needs to address all forms of non-literal language.

4.  **Lack of High-Quality Annotated Data:** High-quality, large-scale datasets for idiom disambiguation are still relatively scarce. Creating such datasets is expensive and time-consuming.

5.  **Compositionality Challenges:** While idioms are *non-compositional* (meaning their meaning isn’t derived from the individual words), they often interact with compositional elements of the sentence. Untangling these interactions is complex.

6.  **Subjectivity & Cultural Nuances:** Idiom interpretation can be subjective and influenced by cultural background. What’s obvious to one person might be confusing to another.

7.  **Evaluating Multimodal Understanding:** Assessing the quality and relevance of visual representations of idioms is challenging. Objective evaluation metrics are still lacking.

**Next Steps for Improving Idiom Interpretation in NLP:**

1.  **Leveraging Larger, More Powerful LLMs:** Continued use of increasingly capable LLMs (like GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro) is essential. These models have greater capacity to capture contextual nuances and generalize to unseen idioms.

2.  **Contrastive Learning & Few-Shot Learning:** Employing contrastive learning techniques to learn robust representations of idioms and their contexts. Few-shot learning allows systems to quickly adapt to novel idioms with only a few examples.

3.  **Knowledge Graph Integration:** Integrating external knowledge graphs (e.g., ConceptNet, WordNet) to provide broader semantic information and contextual knowledge about idioms.

4.  **Commonsense Reasoning:** Incorporating commonsense reasoning capabilities to better understand the implied meaning and context of idioms.

5.  **Multimodal Training:** Training models on datasets that include both text and visual information, encouraging them to learn the connection between linguistic meaning and visual imagery. This goes beyond simply generating images; it requires a deeper understanding of the underlying concepts.

6.  **Explainable AI (XAI) Techniques:** Developing XAI methods to understand *why* a system chose a particular idiom interpretation. This would help identify biases and improve trust in the system.

7.  **Culturally Aware Models:** Developing models that are sensitive to cultural nuances and regional variations in idiom usage. This could involve training models on datasets that represent diverse cultural perspectives.

8.  **Active Learning:**  Employing active learning strategies to selectively annotate the most informative examples, improving the efficiency of data collection.

9. **Dynamic Prompting & Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG):** Combining the strengths of LLMs with external knowledge retrieval systems. For idioms, this could involve retrieving relevant definitions and examples from a knowledge base *during* inference.

**In the long term, the goal is to move beyond simply *identifying* and *disambiguating* idioms to truly *understanding* their meaning and role in communication. This requires building AI systems that can reason about language in a more human-like way – possessing common sense, cultural awareness, and the ability to interpret figurative language with nuance and sensitivity.**"
