## Description
The paper investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) 
can be prompted to perform reasoning without 
any few-shot examples.
By introducing a simple cue&mdash;“Let’s think step by step”&mdash;the authors show that 
LLMs can generate coherent intermediate reasoning in a purely zero-shot setting. 
Through a series of experiments and comparisons against few-shot prompting, 
the paper demonstrates that this cueing is often sufficient 
to turn LLMs into effective _zero-shot reasoners_.

#### Description (LLM)

The paper "Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Reasoners" by Takeshi Kojima, Shixiang Shane Gu, Machel Reid, Yutaka Matsuo, and Yusuke Iwasawa attempts to fill the gap in existing research on the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in zero-shot reasoning.

Zero-shot reasoning refers to the ability of a model to reason and solve problems without having seen any specific examples or training data for the task at hand. This is in contrast to few-shot learning, where the model is given a few examples to learn from, or in-context learning, where the model is trained on a specific task with a set of examples.

Existing research has shown that LLMs are excellent few-shot learners, but their performance on system-2 tasks, which require slow and multi-step reasoning, is limited. System-2 tasks include tasks such as arithmetic, symbolic reasoning, and commonsense reasoning, which are difficult for humans and are considered a grand challenge for LLMs.

The paper identifies a specific gap in existing research, which is the lack of effective methods for zero-shot reasoning in LLMs. While there have been proposals for few-shot and in-context learning, there is a need for methods that can enable LLMs to reason and solve problems without any specific examples or training data.

To address this gap, the authors propose a novel approach to zero-shot chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting for LLMs. The approach involves adding a simple prompt "Let's think step by step" before each answer to facilitate step-by-step thinking and multi-hop reasoning across a wide range of tasks.

The paper shows that this approach can significantly improve the performance of LLMs on zero-shot reasoning tasks, including arithmetic, symbolic, and other logical reasoning tasks. The approach also generates reasonable and understandable chain of thought across diverse tasks, which is an important aspect of human reasoning.

The paper highlights the importance of carefully exploring and analyzing the enormous zero-shot knowledge hidden inside LLMs before crafting finetuning datasets or few-shot exemplars. It also emphasizes the potential of simple prompting methods to elicit high-level, multi-task broad cognitive capabilities in LLMs, such as generic logical reasoning.

Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of natural language processing, demonstrating the potential of zero-shot CoT prompting to improve the reasoning capabilities of large language models. The paper fills a gap in existing research by providing a novel approach to zero-shot reasoning in LLMs and demonstrating its effectiveness on a range of tasks.
