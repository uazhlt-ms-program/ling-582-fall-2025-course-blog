## Description

This paper is about improving NMT for low resource languages where parallel bilingual data are not available or are very limited. The authors use a constrained sampling approach for data augmentation. They guide the process using edit distance and a discriminator model to filter out poor-quality samples. This helps reduce ungrammatical or mismatched sentences and produces cleaner, more accurate training data.

#### What are the authors proposing?
The authors propose a constrained sampling method for data augmentation in neural machine translation (NMT) for low-resource languages (LRLs). Their key innovation is a combination of:

Constrained Sampling: Generating augmented data by sampling sentences based on edit distance (insertions, deletions, substitutions) from the original text, rather than random sampling. This approach prioritizes coherence and context.
Discriminator Submodel: Training a discriminator to filter out low-quality augmented sequences, ensuring the final dataset retains high quality. The discriminator is trained on both real training data and negative samples (e.g., word omissions), enabling it to generalize across diverse LRLs.
Model-Agnostic and Language-Independent: The method is adaptable to various NMT architectures and languages, making it practical for integrating into existing systems.
Their proposal directly addresses the challenge of limited parallel corpora in LRLs by introducing a targeted and evaluated approach to augment data, ensuring translations are both accurate and contextually appropriate.

#### What are the results and how do they compare with competing approaches?
Results and Comparison

The authors conducted experiments comparing their constrained sampling method to existing NMT data augmentation baselines (e.g., swapping, replacing, back-translation) using BLEU, ROUGE, and language perplexity metrics. Key findings include:

BLEU Scores:

Their method consistently outperforms baselines in LRLs (e.g., 27.37 BLEU for augmented source vs. 23.25 for the best baseline on Ug–En).
On WMT and IWSLT corpora, their approach shows improved BLEU scores compared to existing DA methods, demonstrating robustness across datasets.
ROUGE Scores:

Achieves higher precision, recall, and F-scores (e.g., 52.55 for the reference vs. 56.28 for the augmented method on Ug–En).
This indicates better translation fluency and coherence, especially in LRLs where data scarcity is a challenge.
Language Perplexity:

Demonstrates improved fluency and context-awareness, with lower perplexity values (e.g., 18.47 for augmented source vs. 23.28 for the best baseline on Az–En).

The authors optimize the temperature (τ) to 0.85, balancing short and long edit distances, which enhances the effectiveness of the augmented data.

The results are quantified and cross-corroborated across Tanzil, WMT, and IWSLT corpora, ensuring robustness to different language pairs and data sizes.

#### Errors and limitations of the LLM 

_Where did it fall short or make mistakes?_

SmolLM3 did an okay job overall. It got many details wrong. It mixed up some of the numbers from the tables, like saying 27.37 BLEU for Ug–En when that number was actually for Az–En. It also confused a few ROUGE scores and made the improvements sound bigger than they really were. It also paired ROUGE-1 precision and F-scores from different columns, giving an incorrect “52.55 to 56.28” comparison. It also listed some datasets the authors didn’t actually use. 
    At first, when I asked it for a brief summary, it described the method as “constrained back-translation,” but the authors never did back-translation. Their approach was actually constrained sampling with edit-distance-based augmentation. Later, when I asked it to tell me what the paper was about or what the authors were proposing, it changed the answer to constrained sampling. So many weird mistakes.
