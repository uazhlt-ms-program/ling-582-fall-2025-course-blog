## Description

This paper describes a technique known as Stance Reasoner, which seeks to understand people's views in social media communications, specifically whether they are neutral, in favor of, or against an issue.The approach is zero-shot, which implies it can handle novel topics without any training examples, as opposed to relying on large labeled datasets.   The fundamental idea is to explain why a post takes a specific perspective utilizing a reasoning process within a language model.   The authors test this strategy on a number of Twitter datasets and show how explicit reasoning enhances and clarifies the model's predictions.

## Motivation

I chose this paper because it combines three things I’m really interested in social media, models that work on the new topics without training, and LLMs that can “show their work” when they reason.
First, social media is full of short, opinionated posts. Being able to tell if a post is for, against, or neutral about a topic is useful for understanding public opinion, spotting misinformation, and studying online conflict. I wanted to see how current NLP methods handle this kind of real, messy data from Twitter.
Second, I like zero-shot methods, where a model can deal with new topics it has never seen during training. This feels closer to real-world use than models that depend on large labeled datasets for every new topic.
Finally, this paper uses chain-of-thought reasoning and self-consistency the same kinds of prompting tricks I use when working with LLMs. I was curious to see how researchers apply these ideas in a more systematic way: how they design the prompts, how they judge the reasoning, and whether this actually improves results.
Overall, I picked this paper because it matches how I already like to use LLMs and gives me a concrete, research-based example of using reasoning-style prompting for a real NLP task.

2. **Provide transparent decision-making** through step-by-step reasoning chains, enabling users to understand and debug the model's predictions.  
3. **Enhance the model's accuracy** by using self-consistency (multiple completions and majority voting) to mitigate model variability.  
4. **Improve the usability** of zero-shot stance detection for applications in social media, where clarity and transparency are critical.  

By combining PLM-based background knowledge with explicit reasoning, the authors seek to create a method that can handle the complexities of social media texts and adapt to new topics efficiently, without compromising on accuracy or interpretability.
